# SELORA — asset brief

Everything under `public/` is either **real public-domain imagery** or a
**stand-in generated locally** by the scripts in `/tools`. This document says
which is which, where each came from, and — for the stand-ins — what the
commissioned replacement has to be.

v6 reduced the site to one fourteen-second shot, and the manifest with it: the
island's CC0 photogrammetry, its sand materials and the unused star plate are
gone. What is left is the planet and nine clouds.

Paths are declared once, in `lib/assets.ts`. Replacing a file at the same path
is the whole integration step.

---

## 1 · The planet — real, public domain

`public/textures/earth/` is derived from **NASA imagery**, which is in the
public domain and requires no permission or licence. It does not need replacing.

| File | Size | Source |
|---|---|---|
| `albedo.webp` | 4096 × 2048 | Blue Marble Next Generation, August 2004 |
| `albedo-lo.webp` | 2048 × 1024 | the same, for phones |
| `clouds.webp` | 4096 × 2048 RGBA | NASA combined cloud composite |
| `ocean.webp` | 2048 × 1024 | derived from GEBCO bathymetry |
| `normal.webp` | 2048 × 1024 | derived from GEBCO elevation |
| `night.webp` | 2048 × 1024 | VIIRS 2012 city lights |

Regenerate with `node tools/gen-earth-v5.mjs`. It fetches any missing NASA
source itself; `MONTH=200412` selects a different Blue Marble frame.

Notes worth keeping if these are ever rebuilt:

- **The ocean mask is geometric, not chromatic.** Water is where the elevation
  is at sea level, unioned with the flat fill the Blue Marble render uses for
  standing water — which is what supplies the lakes. A colour threshold is
  fooled by deserts and by ice.
- **The land grade is display-referred.** Doing vibrance and contrast in linear
  light drives the near-black rainforest through zero and destroys its blue
  channel.
- **No grain, anywhere.** Denoise runs *before* local contrast so the lift
  cannot amplify speckle, and both are masked to land — ungated they print an
  unsharp halo that reads as a black outline between the Antarctic ice and the
  sea.
- **Blue Marble renders the central Arctic as open ocean in every month.** There
  is no sea ice at the north pole and it is not recoverable by choosing a
  different frame; it needs a separate raster.

## 2 · The clouds — STAND-INS

`public/textures/clouds/obj-*.webp` are nine transparent cutouts generated by
`node tools/gen-cloud-objects.mjs` from a single film-scanned negative
(`public/textures/source/cloud-reference.jpg`). They are the only substantial
imagery in the site that is not real.

**What the replacements have to be.**

Nine cumulus **cutouts**, RGBA, alpha to zero on all four sides — not plates. A
cloud in this site is an object with a silhouette that floats in a solid colour
field, so any alpha that runs to a border shows as a straight edge the moment
the sky behind it is a different blue from the one it was cut out of.

The film draws one of each — `far3`, `mid2`, `near1` — on planes riding six
units in front of the camera through the last three seconds. The other six are
alternates.

| Class | Count | Size | Drawn at | Character |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| far | 3 | ~1400 × 900 | 340% of frame width, 66% into the air | thin torn rafts, flattened, no flat base |
| mid | 3 | ~1800 × 1400 | 72% of frame width, 20% into the air | proper cumulus: flat base, cauliflower top |
| near | 3 | ~2400 × 1800 | 135% of frame width, 5% into the air | large soft masses, optically defocused |

**Deliver the alpha channel at full depth.** The current far plates carry 51 and
72 distinct alpha levels against the mid and near plates' 256, and magnified to
fill the frame those four- and six-code steps read as nested contour rings — a
topographic map across the sky. The shader dithers them away, but the fix
belongs in the file.

- Shot against clear sky, high sun, from below or level — never from above.
- **Blown tops to near 250, cool grey undersides around `#8FA9BE`.** The
  underside is what makes a cumulus a solid; a uniformly white cloud is a
  cotton ball.
- **No sky in the file.** Measured: no pixel in any delivered cutout has a
  blue-minus-red chroma above 50, where the negative's own sky sits at 76.
- **No grain, no added texture, no distressing.** There is no grain anywhere in
  this site. The generated stand-ins were re-grained at output size until v6 —
  `grainAmp` is now 0 for all nine, and `GRAIN_MIN`/`GRAIN_MAX` in the generator
  are both 0 so it cannot return by accident. At the magnification the last two
  seconds put the near plates under, a 9/255 emulsion reads as digital noise
  over the one frame the site is asking to be looked at.
- Deliver 16-bit masters with a real alpha channel; the build encodes to WebP.

## 3 · The stars — geometry, nothing to commission

The field is `components/webgl/CinematicStars.tsx`: three depth bands, one draw
call, one deterministic seed. There is no star plate — `public/textures/space/`
was deleted in v6 along with the last thing that referenced it.

---

## 4 · The mark — vector

`components/ui/sharkPaths.ts` is generated by `node tools/mark.mjs` from a
profile and spliced fins. It is the brand mark, used from 16 px in the navigation
upward, so it stays vector. There is no loader for it to appear in any more.

---

## 5 · Delivery checklist

- [ ] Cutouts have alpha on all four sides; no straight edge reaches any border
- [ ] No sky, no halo, no fringe in any matted file
- [ ] No grain, no noise, no added texture anywhere
- [ ] Equirectangular maps seamless in x — measure column 0 against column W−1
- [ ] Colour maps sRGB; masks, normals and light maps linear
- [ ] Nothing shot from above the cloud; nothing golden-hour unless it is the
      terminator on the planet
- [ ] No people, boats, aircraft, buildings or signage anywhere
